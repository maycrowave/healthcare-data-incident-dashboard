## Glossary

### Regulatory decision categories

These are the three outcomes the model predicts. Definitions come from the ICO directly.

**Informal Action Taken**
We have determined that the breach as reported did not warrant use of our formal powers, such as a fine or reprimand. However, we did feel that some action was required, such as advice given.

> The ICO changed its definitions of *Informal Action Taken* and *No Further Action* in April 2021. There are far more cases recorded as *Informal Action Taken* since that date than before it, compared with *No Further Action* cases.

**Investigation Pursued**
The case is passed to our investigations or cyber investigations teams in order to determine what action, if any, is suitable in a particular case. This may not necessarily lead to a full investigation.

**No Further Action**
The investigations team has determined that no further action is required in response to the report. This could be because the issue did not involve any personal data or did not reach the threshold for action, for example.

> See note above on the April 2021 definition change.

### Feature inputs

These are the breach characteristics the Simulator asks for.

**Incident Category**
Whether the breach involved a cyber-related cause or not.
- *Cyber* — incidents arising from electronic means, such as phishing, ransomware, or unauthorised access via systems.
- *Non Cyber* — incidents arising from human or process causes, such as data sent to the wrong recipient or lost paperwork.

**Incident Type**
The specific kind of incident. Examples include *Phishing*, *Data emailed to incorrect recipient*, *Unauthorised access*, *Ransomware*, *Loss / theft of paperwork or data left in insecure location*, and *Other non-cyber incident*.

**Data Subject Type**
Who the breach affected. Multiple types may be selected if a single breach affected more than one group. Categories include *Patients*, *Employees*, *Children*, *Members of the public*, and *Unknown*, among others.

**Data Type**
What kind of personal data was involved. Multiple types may be selected. Categories include *Basic personal identifiers* (e.g. name, address), *Health data*, *Financial data*, *Identification data*, *Information relating to criminal convictions and offences*, and *Unknown*, among others.

**No. Data Subjects Affected**
The approximate scale of the breach, in ordered bands:
*1 to 9*, *10 to 99*, *100 to 1k*, *1k to 10k*, *10k to 100k*, *100k and above*, *Unknown*.

**Time Taken to Report**
How quickly the breach was reported to the ICO from when the controller became aware of it:
*Less than 24 hours*, *24 hours to 72 hours*, *72 hours to 1 week*, *More than 1 week*.

> Under UK GDPR, controllers must notify the ICO within 72 hours of becoming aware of a personal data breach where feasible.
